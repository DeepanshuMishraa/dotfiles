# Originality and diversity checks

## Meaningful idea dimensions

Compare ideas across:

- customer;
- painful job;
- trigger;
- current workaround;
- product mechanism;
- business model;
- distribution path;
- enabling technology;
- wedge;
- founder advantage.

Two ideas are duplicates when only the name or industry noun changes while the mechanism, buyer, workflow, and distribution remain the same.

## Mutation operators

Create descendants by changing at least two dimensions:

- narrow to a high-value sub-workflow;
- switch user while preserving buyer;
- switch buyer while preserving user;
- move from software to service-assisted software or the reverse;
- change from monitoring to action;
- change from generic tool to system of record;
- change distribution through an embedded partner;
- convert a periodic task into a real-time trigger;
- unbundle one valuable feature;
- combine the mechanism with a new technical enabler.

## Wildcards

A wildcard should violate one emerging preference while respecting the founder's hard constraints. Explain why it deserves consideration.

Do not use wildcards to reintroduce unsafe, prohibited, or explicitly impossible categories.

## Diversity checklist

Before showing a round, verify:

- at least five idea shapes are present in round one;
- no customer dominates more than one third of the round;
- no product mechanism dominates more than one third;
- names are not cosmetic variants;
- rejected traits do not dominate refinement;
- the strongest ideas are not all dependent on the same unsupported signal.
