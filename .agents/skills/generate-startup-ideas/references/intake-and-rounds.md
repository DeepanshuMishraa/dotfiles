# Intake and round mechanics

## Intake

Ask three questions in one message:

1. **Founder advantage** — skills, experience, access, curiosity, credibility, and assets.
2. **Taste** — preferred customers, markets, product forms, business models, and explicit dislikes.
3. **Constraints** — time, budget, team, technical depth, geography, risk tolerance, and desired scale.

Accept incomplete or informal answers. Normalize them without forcing the founder to choose a niche too early.

If the user says "surprise me," record the corresponding field as open rather than inventing a preference.

## Round one: exploration

Generate 12 ideas distributed across at least five different shapes. Suggested mix:

- 3 direct pain solvers;
- 2 workflow automation ideas;
- 2 unbundled or simplified products;
- 2 new-technology combinations;
- 1 service-to-software idea;
- 1 local or vertical idea;
- 1 wildcard.

Avoid producing 12 AI assistants with different nouns.

## Reaction language

Accept natural reactions or the compact format:

- `love` — the founder wants more ideas with similar mechanics or customers;
- `maybe` — promising but needs a change;
- `no` — avoid the relevant traits unless intentionally used as a wildcard.

When a reason is supplied, trust the reason more than inferred tags.

## Round two: refinement

Generate eight ideas:

- four descendants;
- two adjacencies;
- two wildcards.

Every descendant must change at least two meaningful dimensions, such as customer, workflow, wedge, distribution, pricing, or enabling technology.

## Additional rounds

Allow unlimited rounds. Alternate between expansion and convergence:

- expansion adds new shapes and markets;
- convergence sharpens loved mechanisms and removes rejected traits.

After two rounds, recommend selecting one to three finalists, but continue ideating if requested.

## Interaction style

- Keep cards compact.
- Use vivid, ownable names.
- Avoid generic startup jargon.
- Let the founder react quickly.
- Never force a winner before validation.
