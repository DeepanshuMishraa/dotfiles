# Evidence and citation rules

## Evidence states

Label every material signal as one of:

- **Observed** — directly supported by a linked public source.
- **Inferred** — a reasonable interpretation of observed evidence.
- **Exploratory** — an idea-generation hypothesis without sufficient evidence.
- **Unknown** — important information that public research did not establish.

Never convert inferred or exploratory claims into observed facts later in the report.

## Source ledger fields

Record:

- source ID;
- URL;
- page or post title;
- source type;
- visible publication or update date;
- access date;
- actor and workflow;
- concise signal summary;
- evidence state;
- idea IDs informed by the source.

## Source quality

Prefer:

1. primary pages and official documentation;
2. direct first-person public statements;
3. original reviews and issue threads;
4. reputable analysis with transparent sources;
5. search snippets only as discovery aids.

Open the original page before relying on a search snippet.

## Claims requiring restraint

Do not claim the following without direct support:

- total addressable market;
- willingness to pay;
- acquisition cost;
- retention;
- customer intent;
- revenue;
- lack of competitors;
- legal or regulatory compliance;
- technical feasibility.

Use "hypothesis," "proxy," or "unknown" when appropriate.

## Quotes and privacy

Paraphrase by default. Use only short excerpts when the exact language matters and link the original source. Do not collect private contact details, sensitive traits, leaked information, or content behind access controls.

## Freshness

Display source dates when visible. Mark undated or stale signals. For technology, regulation, pricing, and product availability, verify current state before finalizing.
