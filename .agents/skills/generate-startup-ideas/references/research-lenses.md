# Public-web research lenses

Use a mixed-source scan. Choose lenses that match the founder profile and hunt mode.

## 1. Complaint and request lens

Look for repeated first-person frustration, requests for alternatives, missing features, cancellations, and "how do I" questions.

Useful sources include public forums, Reddit, Hacker News, product communities, public X posts, and vendor forums.

## 2. Manual-work lens

Look for spreadsheets, templates, copy-paste processes, virtual assistants, agencies, recurring checklists, and tutorial-heavy workflows.

Useful sources include template libraries, public SOPs, YouTube descriptions, help articles, job posts, and community questions.

## 3. Spending lens

Look for existing budgets: paid software, contractors, consultants, agencies, headcount, certification, compliance work, and expensive errors.

Treat a spending proxy as evidence of budget allocation, not proof that buyers will switch.

## 4. Competitor-gap lens

Look for negative reviews, feature requests, migration questions, pricing frustration, integration gaps, and products that have become too broad.

Use official changelogs, public roadmaps, review platforms, app-store reviews, GitHub issues, and community discussions.

## 5. New-enabler lens

Look for newly released APIs, models, devices, standards, platform capabilities, open-source projects, and cost reductions that change what can be built.

Confirm capability and release information with primary documentation.

## 6. Job-to-product lens

Look for repeated roles or job descriptions dominated by a narrow workflow. Extract tasks rather than assuming the role should be replaced.

## 7. Open-source demand lens

Look for popular repositories with recurring hosted-service requests, difficult setup, missing collaboration, enterprise needs, or maintenance gaps.

Stars alone are not commercial demand.

## 8. Regulatory and timing lens

Look for new reporting obligations, platform policy changes, industry standards, deadlines, and workflow changes.

Use primary regulatory or platform sources for material claims.

## Query planning

For a first burst, use at least four query angles and three source types. For finalist validation, use at least:

- two independent problem signals;
- one workaround or spending signal;
- one competitor or substitute source;
- one disconfirming or uncertainty search.

Do not count copied posts or syndicated stories as independent evidence.
